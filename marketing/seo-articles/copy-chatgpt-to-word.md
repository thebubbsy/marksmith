# How to copy ChatGPT into Word without breaking the formatting

If you use ChatGPT, Gemini, or Claude for work, you already know the frustration: the AI generates a brilliant response, you click "Copy", and paste it into Microsoft Word. Instantly, your document is a mess.

The formatting is broken. The text is littered with random asterisks (`**bold**`), weird citation brackets (`[7]`), and code block backgrounds that you can't seem to delete. Instead of getting back to work, you're spending ten minutes manually deleting em-dashes and reformatting headings just to make the text look presentable.

Here is why this happens, the manual way to fix it, and the one-click solution that completely solves the problem.

## Why does pasting from ChatGPT break Word?

AI assistants format their text using **Markdown**, a lightweight markup language. When you copy text directly from the browser, you aren't just copying text—you're copying the browser's HTML rendering of that Markdown.

Microsoft Word tries its best to translate this HTML into its own internal format (OOXML), but it often fails. The result? 
- Headings don't register in your Table of Contents.
- Bullet points have incorrect indentations.
- Tables split awkwardly across pages.
- "Copy code" buttons or black backgrounds get pasted directly into your document.
- Citations and AI-specific artifacts (like excessive em-dashes) come along for the ride.

## The Manual Fix: Paste as Plain Text

The most common workaround is to use **Paste as Plain Text** (or `Ctrl + Shift + V`). This strips away all the messy HTML and leaves you with just the text.

However, this creates a new problem: you lose *all* formatting.
- Headings become regular text.
- Bulleted lists become regular text.
- Tables completely break into unreadable lines of words.
- Bold and italic emphasis is lost.

You then have to spend time manually re-applying all the formatting in Word, which completely defeats the purpose of using AI to save time.
